# Arboleda-Serna et al. (2019): short intervals versus continuous training

**Source type:** randomized controlled trial. **Coverage:** methods, results and limitations inspected; references indexed only as bibliography. **Population:** 44 healthy men aged 18–44, below 150 minutes/week of aerobic activity, randomized 22 per arm; screened and supervised. This is not a trial of established competitive runners.

[Original PDF](../../resources/sources/arboleda-serna-2019-hiit-vs-continuous-training.pdf) · [DOI: 10.7705/biomedica.4451](https://doi.org/10.7705/biomedica.4451) · [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7357372/)

Arboleda-Serna VH, Feito Y, Patiño-Villada FA, Vargas-Romero AV, Arango-Vélez EF. *Biomédica* 39:524–536. Issue year 2019; the PDF also lists online publication in December 2018. PDF page 1 is printed page 524.

## Protocol and numbers

These are experimental treatments, not default workouts for the user.

| Item | Study definition | Locator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Schedule | 3 supervised sessions/week for 8 weeks, 24 planned sessions | Printed 526 / PDF 3 |

The abstract reports a slightly different estimate: 1.01 mL/kg/min (95% CI −2.16 to 4.18). The reason for this discrepancy is not established here. Preserve the locator and analysis specification with any estimate; do not mix them.

## What it supports

HIIT improved within its group, but the between-group contrast did not establish superiority. A significant change in one group and a nonsignificant change in another is not itself evidence of a difference. The confidence interval also does not establish equivalence. Continuous training produced lower post-intervention systolic pressure; this is a separate endpoint and does not justify predicting an individual's blood-pressure response. [Printed 528–529 / PDF 5–6.]

Baseline age and body composition differed between arms; models adjusted for baseline values and covariates. Missing outcomes used multiple imputation; a sensitivity analysis required at least 70% attendance. The resistance co-intervention, supervised incline treadmill setting, small male sample and eight-week duration limit transfer to outdoor recreational running. [Printed 527–528 / PDF 4–5.]

## Spotter use and required inputs

Use when explaining why “HIIT” needs a specific work/recovery protocol, and why one interval trial cannot establish a universally best workout. Record repetitions, work and recovery durations, incline, intensity basis and adherence before making a protocol comparison.

The authors selected recovery speed from VO2 testing because heart rate would not fall quickly enough during a short recovery to represent its intensity reliably. Do not classify each short interval solely by instantaneous watch HR. [Printed 526 / PDF 3.]

No user-level predictive formula is extracted. Garmin VO2 estimates are not the laboratory gas-analysis outcome used here. Revisit the original methods before implementing a calculator or workout template.
