# Mountjoy et al.: 2023 IOC REDs consensus

- **Source:** *2023 International Olympic Committee's (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)*, British Journal of Sports Medicine, pp. 1073–1098.
- **Original:** [local PDF](../../resources/sources/mountjoy-2023-ioc-reds-consensus.pdf).
- **Publisher:** [DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994).
- **Version note:** The PDF states first publication on 26 September 2023 but its footer cites 2024; retain the title's 2023 source identifier rather than silently treating those dates as equivalent.
- **Type:** Expert consensus and clinical framework drawing on research, not an individual diagnostic test.
- **Coverage:** Targeted reading of definition/formula, evidence context, energy-availability limitations, and diagnosis: printed pp. 1073–1074, 1080 and 1086 (PDF pp. 1–2, 8 and 14). Clinical tables and supplements are not fully indexed; no clinical scoring algorithm was extracted.

## Findings and formula

REDs concerns impaired health or performance associated with **problematic** low energy availability. The consensus distinguishes potentially adaptable exposure from problematic exposure; a brief deficit is not by itself a diagnosis. See definitions, printed p. 1074 (PDF p. 2).

`EA = (energy_intake_kcal_per_day − exercise_energy_expenditure_kcal_per_day) / FFM_kg`

- Output: kcal/kg fat-free mass/day.
- Source: printed p. 1073 (PDF p. 1).
- Exercise expenditure is not total daily energy expenditure. Substituting total expenditure changes the meaning of the formula.
- Intake, expenditure and fat-free mass must refer to compatible measurement periods and methods.
- Free-living measurement has substantial burden and error. This is a conceptual/research quantity, not a reliable calculation from watch calories plus scale weight (printed p. 1080; PDF p. 8).

## Limits that must travel with the numbers

The historical **30 kcal/kg FFM/day** threshold arose from short laboratory studies in small samples of sedentary women. The consensus warns against a single universal clinical cutoff: effects differ between people, sexes and physiological systems (printed p. 1080; PDF p. 8). Do not turn this number into a green/red status indicator or dietary target.

Diagnosis requires expert physician assessment, information from a multidisciplinary team, and exclusion of other explanations. Clinical history, examination and indicated laboratory/imaging data contribute; symptoms alone are not specific (printed p. 1086; PDF p. 14). The evidence discussed in the introduction is predominantly female and often cross-sectional, limiting causal and population-wide inferences (printed p. 1073; PDF p. 1).

## Use in Spotter

This source helps explain why persistent fatigue, poor recovery or declining performance deserve context and sometimes clinical assessment. It does **not** validate REDs diagnosis from Garmin Body Battery, heart rate, VO2 estimates, steps, weight change or a calculated calorie deficit. That restriction is an application of the clinical requirements above, not a Garmin validation result reported by this paper.

Keep the clinical assessment tool with qualified clinicians. Spotter can preserve the user's observations and encourage appropriate assessment, without assigning a REDs score or a diet intended to treat it.
