# HHS (2018): Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans

- **Source:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, *Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans*, second edition (2018).
- **Original:** [local PDF](../../resources/sources/hhs-2018-physical-activity-guidelines-2ed.pdf).
- **Publisher PDF:** [health.gov](https://health.gov/sites/default/files/2019-09/Physical_Activity_Guidelines_2nd_edition.pdf).
- **Type:** Population health guidance, not a sport-performance training plan.
- **Coverage:** Adult chapter, printed/PDF pp. 56–62, and step-count discussion, pp. 111–112. Printed and PDF page numbers match in this copy. Guidance for children, pregnancy, older adults, disability and chronic conditions is not comprehensively indexed here.

## Adult guidance

The adult chapter covers ages 18–64. These recommendations concern substantial health benefits; benefits can occur below these amounts, and the range is not a performance optimum (p. 56).

| Quantity | Interpretation | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 150–300 min/week moderate aerobic activity, or 75–150 min/week vigorous activity, or an equivalent mixture | Spread activity through the week. Additional activity can provide further benefit. | p. 56 |
| Strength work on ≥2 days/week | Moderate or greater effort involving all major muscle groups. Session count alone cannot establish coverage or effort. | pp. 56, 61 |
| Moderate intensity: 3.0–5.9 METs; vigorous: ≥6.0 METs | Absolute intensity classification; relative effort depends on fitness. Do not infer METs from steps alone. | p. 58 |
| Any duration of moderate/vigorous activity contributes | No minimum ten-minute bout is required by this edition. | pp. 57–58 |

## Simple accounting

`moderate_equivalent_minutes = moderate_minutes + 2 × vigorous_minutes`

This is an arithmetic expression of the guideline's approximate 2:1 equivalence (p. 58), not a training-load formula. Inputs require classified aerobic minutes over the same week. Do not double-count overlapping imported and manually logged sessions. Missing intensity means an unknown contribution.

For conversational intensity checks, the guideline describes moderate activity as allowing speech but not singing; vigorous activity generally allows only a few words before a breath (p. 60). This is a rule of thumb, not a measured heart-rate zone or a replacement for individual context.

## Steps

The source explicitly says that **10,000 steps/day is not itself a guideline** (p. 112). Step totals include light activity and do not directly establish moderate/vigorous minutes. Its worked example uses a person's own observed walking steps to add a walking goal to baseline activity; it does not establish a universal steps-to-minutes conversion (pp. 111–112).

## Use in Spotter

Useful for discussing walking and weekly activity in everyday terms. Garmin daily steps can show a trend without implying that a clinical or aerobic target was met. A future weekly summary could report classified aerobic minutes and known strength days separately, with incomplete data visible. This source alone does not validate Garmin's intensity-minute calculation.
