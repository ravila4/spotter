# Helms et al. (2014): protein during caloric restriction

- **Source:** *A Systematic Review of Dietary Protein During Caloric Restriction in Resistance Trained Lean Athletes: A Case for Higher Intakes*. International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism 24, 127–138.
- **Original:** [local PDF](../../resources/sources/helms-2014-protein-during-caloric-restriction.pdf).
- **Provenance:** [DOI: 10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092765/).
- **Type:** Systematic review with an author-proposed intake range; not a trial of the complete recommended range.
- **Coverage:** Abstract, population criteria, rationale, limitations, and conclusions, printed pp. 127–128 and 135 (PDF pp. 1–2 and 9). Individual study tables and forest-plot estimates have not been independently extracted. Search cutoff: July 2013; no claim of a current comprehensive evidence review.

## Findings and numbers

| Finding | Applicability and uncertainty | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six studies, containing 13 groups, met inclusion criteria. | Adults at least 18 years old, more than six months of resistance training, energy restriction and continued resistance training. Body-fat eligibility limits were ≤23% for men and ≤35% for women; these are study selection criteria, not recommended body-fat targets. | Printed p. 127; PDF p. 1 |
| Authors propose 2.3–3.1 g protein/kg **fat-free mass/day**. | Intended for lean resistance-trained athletes in a calorie deficit; authors suggest the higher end with greater leanness or restriction. It is not a universal maintenance-diet target or an exact dose-response equation. | Printed pp. 127 and 135; PDF pp. 1 and 9 |
| Only two included studies compared differing protein intakes with well-matched groups and appropriate controls. | Diet, body fat, training stimulus, and intervention duration confound comparisons across the remaining studies. Higher protein should not be treated as guaranteeing preservation of lean tissue. | Printed p. 135; PDF p. 9 |
| Raising protein can displace carbohydrate and fat within a fixed energy intake. | Authors discuss performance and hormonal concerns when other macronutrients become insufficient; this review alone cannot set an individual's whole diet. | Printed p. 135; PDF p. 9 |

## Calculation candidate

`protein_g_per_day = FFM_kg × selected_g_per_kg_FFM_per_day`

The coefficient range above comes from the review; multiplication is its arithmetic application. **FFM is not total body mass.** A scale weight alone does not supply the required input. If body-fat fraction is measured, `FFM_kg = body_mass_kg × (1 − body_fat_fraction)` is a derived identity; the uncertainty of the body-composition measurement propagates into the result. Do not invent that fraction.

## Use in Spotter

Load this note when explaining why protein discussions depend on calorie deficit, training experience, and body composition. Before personalizing, establish those inputs and the user's goal. Do not automatically calculate a target from a stored weight, prescribe weight loss, or present this older review as a complete current nutrition recommendation.
