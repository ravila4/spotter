## Numbers and formulas

| Item | Definition or guidance | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repetition volume | `sum(reps)` across included sets | Printed p. 451 / PDF p. 467 |
| Volume-load | `sum(load × reps)` across included sets | Printed p. 451 / PDF p. 467 |
| Average load per repetition | `volume_load / repetition_volume`, provided repetition volume is positive | Printed p. 451 / PDF p. 467 |
| Progression heuristic | Exceed the assigned goal by at least two reps in the final set on two consecutive workouts, then consider increasing load | Printed p. 459 / PDF p. 475 |
| Relative increment | 2.5–10% is offered as an alternative to absolute equipment increments | Printed p. 459 / PDF p. 475; depends on exercise and training status |

The progression heuristic is more specific about the final set than [ACSM (2009)](acsm-2009-resistance-training-progression.md); keep the two formulations distinct.

## Calculation boundaries

With load recorded in kg, volume-load is conventionally reported in kg or kg·repetitions; average load has units kg. Convert lb before mixing records. Volume-load omits displacement and is not mechanical work in joules, calories, or a measure of hypertrophy. The text discusses its relationship to work, but Spotter should not label it as measured energy or power.

Identical totals can arise from different set structures and exertion levels. Compare similar exercises and equipment, and preserve dumbbell load conventions. The book discusses including body mass when system mass moves; that does not justify automatically adding full bodyweight to every exercise. Bodyweight-only sets with unknown external load should remain unknown for external volume-load, not zero.

## Use in Spotter

Recorded load, reps, exercise identity, and units can support descriptive volume-load trends. Report incomplete sets rather than silently treating missing values as zero. A progression conversation additionally requires intended repetitions, final-set identity, comparable consecutive workouts, available increments, and the user's reported difficulty. These formulas and heuristics are knowledge candidates; indexing them does not implement calculations or authorize changing a workout plan.
