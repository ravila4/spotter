# ACSM (2009): resistance training progression

- **Source:** American College of Sports Medicine; Ratamess NA and colleagues. *Progression Models in Resistance Training for Healthy Adults*. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 41(3):687–708 (2009).
- **Identifier:** [DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670).
- **Original:** [Local PDF](../../resources/sources/acsm-2009-resistance-training-progression.pdf), available separately from Git.
- **Type and population:** Position stand for healthy adults, differentiated by training experience and goal; not an individual treatment plan.
- **Coverage:** Summary, strength loading/progression discussion, and recommendation tables inspected. PDF page 1 is printed page 687; printed page = PDF page + 686.
- **Review status:** Historical guidance. ACSM's [2026 position stand](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897) explicitly updates this document; its existence and update relationship were verified through [ACSM](https://acsm.org/science-spotlight-acsm-releases-new-position-stand-on-resistance-training/) and [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) on 2026-09-12. The newer stand has not been fully indexed here. Do not present these 2009 ranges as current consensus without reviewing that update.

## Useful guidance from this edition

| Item | Source recommendation | Qualification and location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial strength loading | 60–70% of one-repetition maximum (1RM), 8–12 repetitions | Novice/intermediate healthy adults; printed p. 690 / PDF p. 4. An RM describes capacity, not merely a recorded rep count. |
| Progression trigger | Increase load by 2–10% after exceeding the intended repetitions by 1–2 on two consecutive sessions | Smaller increments for smaller muscle mass exercises; evidence category B; printed p. 690 / PDF p. 4. The abstract omits the consecutive-session condition. |
| Frequency | Novices 2–3, intermediate 3–4, advanced 4–5 training days/week | Program-level guidance, not a requirement to train every muscle on every day; printed p. 687 / PDF p. 1. |
| Experience definitions | Intermediate roughly six months of consistent training; advanced years | This source's categories differ from Rhea's one-year cutoff; printed p. 687 / PDF p. 1. |

## Calculation candidate

For a chosen increment `p`, `proposed_load = current_load × (1 + p/100)`. This is arithmetic implementing the source's percentage recommendation, not a prediction of the user's capacity. Preserve kg or lb consistently and consider available equipment increments. Never apply the trigger merely because two logged sets have more repetitions than a previous set: the planned target and comparable consecutive sessions are required.

## Use in Spotter

This can support a conversation about whether a familiar exercise is ready for a small load increase. Required context includes the user's goal, intended rep target, equipment, relevant recent sessions, and reported difficulty. Recorded completed reps alone do not establish an RM. The recommendation is a candidate for discussion, not an automatic database update.

## Limits and related sources

These prescriptions summarize evidence available in 2009; exact dose ranges should not become universal thresholds. The stand itself requires attention to goals, capacity, and training status. Compare [Rhea (2003)](rhea-2003-strength-dose-response-meta-analysis.md) for the older dose-response evidence and [Haff and Triplett](haff-triplett-essentials-strength-conditioning-4ed.md) for a related, but different, progression heuristic.
