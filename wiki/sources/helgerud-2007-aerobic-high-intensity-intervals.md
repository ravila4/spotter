# Helgerud et al. (2007): aerobic intervals

**Source type:** randomized training experiment. **Coverage:** recruitment, interventions, principal results and measurement limitations inspected. **Population:** healthy male university students already doing endurance or leisure activity at least three times/week. Of 55 recruited, 40 completed the analysis after dropout and attendance exclusions; mean age 24.6 years. [Printed 666 / PDF 2.]

[Original PDF](../../resources/sources/helgerud-2007-aerobic-high-intensity-intervals.pdf) · [DOI: 10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/)

Helgerud J et al. *Aerobic High-Intensity Intervals Improve VO2max More Than Moderate Training*. *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise* 39(4):665–671. PDF page 1 is printed page 665.

## Protocols

All groups trained three times/week for eight weeks on a treadmill at 5.3% incline. The investigators aimed to match total work/oxygen consumption. These are study arms, not four interchangeable prescriptions. [Printed 667–668 / PDF 3–4.]

| Arm | Main work | Recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous low intensity | 45 minutes at 70% HRmax | Continuous |
| Study's lactate-threshold arm | 24.25 minutes at 85% HRmax | Continuous |
| Short intervals | 47 × 15 seconds at 90–95% HRmax | 15 seconds at warm-up velocity, corresponding to 70% HRmax |
| Long intervals | 4 × 4 minutes at 90–95% HRmax | 3 minutes active recovery at 70% HRmax between bouts |

Arms 2–4 included 10 minutes warm-up and 3 minutes cool-down at 70% HRmax. HRmax came from maximal testing. During 4-minute bouts, participants reached the target after about 1–1.5 minutes; the target was not an instruction to sprint immediately until HR caught up. Short intervals reached target after about 3–4 minutes of the sequence. [Printed 667–668 / PDF 3–4.]

## Findings and limits

Absolute VO2max improved more in interval arms than continuous arms, without a significant difference between the two interval arms. Reported gains of 5.5% and 7.2% refer to **absolute VO2max in L/min**, not percent change calculated from the abstract's body-mass-relative group means. Running economy improved in every arm. [Printed 668 / PDF 4, Figure 2; printed 669 / PDF 5, Table 1.]

This supports the effectiveness of these intervals under these conditions. It does not establish that 4 × 4 is uniquely best, that three hard sessions/week is appropriate for a new runner, or that watch VO2 should rise by 7.2%. Attrition and exclusion for attendance below 90% constrain generalization; cardiac output/stroke volume analyses used only six participants per group. [Printed 666 / PDF 2.]

## Spotter use and required inputs

Use for a worked example of interval structure, or to compare a recorded workout with the experimental protocol. Required inputs: measured/estimated HRmax with provenance, interval timing, recovery, treadmill incline or terrain, recent training and reported tolerance. An average heart rate cannot reconstruct time at target intensity.

Simple arithmetic, if explicitly requested: target HR in beats/min = selected HRmax fraction × HRmax in beats/min. This is percentage of maximum HR, **not heart-rate reserve**. Do not replace measured maximum with an age formula without labeling that additional assumption.

Garmin VO2 observations and lab measurements must remain distinct. This paper supplies neither a Garmin calibration nor an individual forecast.
