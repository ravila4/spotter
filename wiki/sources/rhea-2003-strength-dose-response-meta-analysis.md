# Rhea et al. (2003): strength dose response

- **Source:** Rhea MR, Alvar BA, Burkett LN, Ball SD. *A Meta-analysis to Determine the Dose Response for Strength Development*. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 35(3):456–464 (2003).
- **Identifier:** [DOI: 10.1249/01.MSS.0000053727.63505.D4](https://doi.org/10.1249/01.MSS.0000053727.63505.D4).
- **Original:** [Local PDF](../../resources/sources/rhea-2003-strength-dose-response-meta-analysis.pdf), available separately from Git.
- **Type and coverage:** Historical meta-analysis; abstract, methods, results, and dose-response discussion inspected (printed pp. 456–459 / PDF pp. 1–4). Printed page = PDF page + 455.
- **Population:** 140 studies and 1,433 effect sizes across training populations. “Trained” required at least one year of previous weight training (printed pp. 456–457 / PDF pp. 1–2).

## Findings to retain with their definitions

| Variable | Highest mean response reported | What the number means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relative load | Untrained: 60% 1RM; trained: 80% 1RM | Mean program intensity, not a mandated load for every set. |
| Frequency | Untrained: 3 days/week; trained: 2 days/week | Days training a particular muscle group, not total gym visits. |
| Volume | 4 sets per muscle group per workout in both groups | Not four sets per exercise and not four weekly sets. |

Locations: coding definitions on printed p. 457 / PDF p. 2; comparisons and discussion on printed pp. 458–459 / PDF pp. 3–4. These are peaks among pooled categories, not proof of a uniquely optimal combined prescription for an individual.

## Formula and interpretation

The analysis begins with `ES = (posttest mean − pretest mean) / pretest SD`, then applies a sample-size bias adjustment (printed p. 457 / PDF p. 2). ES is dimensionless; pre/post strength measurements must use matching units. The correction factor is not explicitly specified in the inspected passage, so this note is insufficient to reproduce the complete analysis.

This is a within-group standardized change, not automatically a treatment-versus-control effect. It is not suitable as a personal progress score from one user's two workouts: a study-group standard deviation is part of the definition.

## Limits

- Dose bins contain uneven numbers of effect sizes, a limitation the authors identify (printed p. 458 / PDF p. 3).
- Only 21 studies involved people older than 55, 13 involved competitive athletes, and six involved participants younger than 18 (same page).
- Comparisons pool different interventions rather than randomizing one population across every dose combination. Apparent peaks can reflect differences between studies; they do not establish an individual causal optimum.
- Training classifications differ from [ACSM (2009)](acsm-2009-resistance-training-progression.md). Do not merge categories without checking definitions. This is historical evidence, not an up-to-date review.

## Use in Spotter

Useful for explaining why training experience and muscle-group accounting matter. Before comparing a user's program, obtain training history and map exercises to muscle groups with explicit overlap rules. Spotter's exercise set count should not silently become a muscle-group dose. Use the paper as context for discussion, not an automatic “four sets is best” rule. See the newer-review pointer in the [ACSM note](acsm-2009-resistance-training-progression.md).
