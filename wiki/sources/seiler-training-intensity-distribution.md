## Useful interpretation

Much of the evidence concerns trained national/international endurance athletes with substantial training volume, across running, skiing, cycling and other sports. Their common emphasis on low intensity supports asking how easy and hard work fit together over time. It does not prove that every recreational runner needs exactly 80% easy minutes, or that the same distribution is optimal at two sessions per week. [Printed 32–35 / PDF 4–7.]

The chapter's summary suggests about two high-intensity sessions weekly as a useful balance in its endurance context. Treat this as author synthesis, conditioned on background training and tolerance, rather than a minimum dose or automatic scheduling rule. Its discussion also stresses an established endurance base before substantial increases in intensity. [Printed 38 / PDF 10.]

## Potential calculations

These are transparent bookkeeping definitions for Spotter, not new physiological models:

- Session share (%) = 100 × sessions assigned to a category / all classified sessions.
- Time share (%) = 100 × minutes in a category / all classified minutes.

Always label which denominator is used. Report unclassified sessions/minutes separately. Warm-up and recovery can make an interval session mostly low-intensity minutes while its session purpose remains high intensity. No fixed HR-to-lactate conversion is extracted.

The classified total must be positive. If nothing is classified, the share is
unknown, not zero percent.

## Spotter use and missing inputs

Use to explain intensity terminology and summarize a block of recorded running once session purpose or detailed samples are available. Ask for purpose and effort when unclear. Current summary pace or average HR alone cannot recover a full intensity distribution. Use known individual thresholds when available; avoid inventing them from a zone label.

Before treating 80/20 as a current consensus or implementing automatic coaching rules, review newer intervention/systematic-review evidence and the user's actual training volume. The incomplete chapter bibliography remains an indexing issue, not evidence that the chapter is recent.
