## Selected insights

E, M, T, I and R mean easy, marathon pace, threshold, interval and repetition training. They encode training purposes in this coaching system. They are not Garmin zone numbers or synonyms for Seiler's three-zone categories. [PDF 82.]

Daniels describes easy running as conversational and comfortable, used for ordinary mileage, warm-ups, recovery jogging and long runs. He gives typical intensity ranges of 59–74% VO2max or 65–79% HRmax. These are author guidelines, not individualized physiological breakpoints, and the two percentages use different denominators. [PDF 83–85; printed topic starts 50.]

The author's suggestion to hold weekly running volume for roughly 3–4 weeks before increasing contrasts with an automatic weekly percentage increase. It is a coaching approach, not a tested universal injury-prevention threshold. The same discussion includes duration suggestions that should not become minimum eligibility rules for beginners or walking/running sessions. [PDF 84–85.]

## VDOT: useful concept, incomplete calculator specification

VDOT estimates running ability from performance using a representative running-economy relation and the fraction of maximum associated with race duration. It is a calculated, performance-based value; individual running economy helps explain disagreement with directly measured VO2max. [PDF 115–116; printed 74–76 topic.]

Conceptual relationship:

`VDOT = estimated oxygen demand at race speed / modeled fraction sustained for race duration`

The numerator uses mL/kg/min and the denominator is dimensionless. This note does **not** supply the fitted curves, coefficients, pace tables or complete algorithm. Do not implement a calculator from this relationship alone or infer coefficients from a graph. [PDF 116–118.]

Race distance, elapsed race time, date, conditions, and whether the effort represents current ability are needed. A short-race estimate is not proof of readiness for a marathon; specificity and conditions affect transfer. [PDF 118.]

## Spotter use and boundaries

Use this note to distinguish workout purposes and to explain why a recent race can inform running pace. Confirm the runner's goal, training history and relevant race before offering progression. Preserve the source and meaning of each metric: **Garmin VO2 is not Daniels VDOT**, and a Garmin number must not be inserted into VDOT pace tables merely because the numerical ranges look similar.
