# Running intensity

The collection contains controlled interval trials, a synthesis of endurance
training practice, and a coaching textbook. They address different questions.

| Source | Contribution | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Helgerud 2007](../sources/helgerud-2007-aerobic-high-intensity-intervals.md) | Interval protocols improved laboratory VO2 in the studied setting | Small sample of active men; no individual prediction or proof that 4 × 4 is uniquely best |
| [Arboleda-Serna 2019](../sources/arboleda-serna-2019-hiit-vs-continuous-training.md) | Short-interval and continuous protocols compared in less-active men | No established between-group VO2 superiority; this also does not prove equivalence |
| [Seiler](../sources/seiler-training-intensity-distribution.md) | Definitions and distribution of easy/hard training | Much evidence concerns trained athletes; sessions and minutes are different denominators |
| [Daniels](../sources/daniels-2022-running-formula-4ed.md) | Workout purposes and performance-based VDOT | Selected textbook coverage; complete pace calculation is not extracted |

## Why the trials do not yield one universal workout

The interval trials used different populations, work/recovery patterns, incline,
and surrounding training. A common label such as HIIT hides those differences.
Their outcomes should be read with the protocol and analysis, not combined into
a promise of a particular VO2 increase. Helgerud's reported percentage increases
refer to absolute VO2, while Garmin's stored reports use body-mass-relative units.

## Zones need definitions

Seiler's three-zone model, the chapter's five-zone model, and Daniels' workout
letters are separate systems. A zone number alone is not enough to translate
between them. Individual breakpoints, sport, and measurement method matter.

Garmin zone boundaries and activity time in zones are a useful import candidate.
The [Spotter mapping](using-with-spotter.md) records the data to verify before
implementation. An activity's average HR cannot recover its intensity distribution.

For an intensity summary, state whether the denominator is classified sessions
or classified minutes. Warm-up and recovery can occupy much of a hard session's
time. Seiler's approximate 80/20 session distribution must not silently become
an 80/20 minutes rule. Unclassified activity remains visible.

## Running ability and watch observations

Daniels' VDOT is performance-based and depends on modeled running economy and
race duration. Garmin VO2 reports are a different metric. The source note
explains the distinction, but does not contain a complete VDOT calculator.
Do not feed a Garmin value into a pace table on the assumption that the two
metrics are interchangeable.

Useful questions for future coaching include the purpose of a session, recent
race performance, training volume, interval structure, conditions, and reported
effort. An imported number complements this context; it does not supply it.
