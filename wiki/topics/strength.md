# Strength training

The sources help describe a program and discuss progression. They do not identify
a single dose that is best for every person.

## Definitions that change the answer

Rhea counts frequency and sets by muscle group. Spotter records exercises and
sets. Those are different quantities: compound exercises overlap, and allocating
their sets to muscles requires a stated convention. A weekly exercise count
cannot be compared directly with sets per muscle per workout.

The sources also define training experience differently. ACSM's intermediate
category begins around six months of consistent training, while Rhea's trained
group requires at least a year. Combining their ranges without these definitions
would hide a population difference.

## Progression

Both the historical ACSM guidance and the textbook consider repeated performance
above an assigned repetition goal, but their triggers differ. The textbook
specifies the final set and at least two extra repetitions; ACSM describes one
or two extra repetitions. Both require two consecutive sessions. The exact
wording and pages are in the linked notes.

Before proposing an increase, establish the goal, intended reps, comparable
equipment, recent sessions, reported effort, and available weight increments.
Two completed sets alone do not establish that either trigger was met. Review
the newer ACSM guidance before adopting the older percentages as a standing rule.

## Describing progress

Volume-load can summarize comparable loaded sets, but it is not mechanical work
or energy expenditure. It does not account for range of motion, effort, or how
repetitions are distributed. Missing loads stay unknown. The
[numerical reference](numbers.md) links to the definitions and required inputs.

These comparisons can support conversation now. Automatic progression and
muscle-group accounting are not implemented by this wiki.
