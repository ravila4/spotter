# Nutrition and recovery

The two sources answer different questions. [Helms et al.](../sources/helms-2014-protein-during-caloric-restriction.md)
discuss protein during energy restriction in trained, lean adults.
[The IOC consensus](../sources/mountjoy-2023-ioc-reds-consensus.md) discusses
problematic low energy availability and clinical assessment. Neither establishes
that the user should lose weight.

## Numbers with restricted applicability

| Quantity | Meaning | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Helms protein range | 2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass/day | Author-proposed range from a small, older review; specific population and deficit context |
| Energy availability | Intake minus exercise expenditure, divided by fat-free mass | Research quantity with substantial free-living measurement error |
| Historical 30 kcal/kg FFM/day threshold | A threshold from particular laboratory evidence | IOC cautions against treating it as a universal clinical cutoff |

Fat-free mass (FFM) is not scale weight. The protein range cannot be multiplied
by the stored body weight without changing its definition. Energy availability
also uses exercise expenditure, not total daily expenditure. Formula details and
source pages are in the linked notes and [numerical index](numbers.md).

## Implications for conversation

Ask about the person's goal and establish whether the source's population and
conditions apply before discussing an intake range. The Helms review does not
provide a complete current diet prescription, and its range is not a guarantee
of preserving lean tissue.

The IOC guidance gives a reason to take persistent fatigue and poor recovery
seriously while considering other explanations. Garmin observations and weight
trends do not supply the clinical information required to diagnose REDs. The
wiki does not extract a clinical scoring tool or turn the historical threshold
into a traffic-light status.
